# The $10K Checklist

Score the first working preview and the polished result on these eight outcomes, from one to ten. Be strict and specific. All production levels share the standard; more expensive generation alone earns no points. Use the complete desktop and phone experience, actual film playback and functioning controls as evidence, not screenshots alone.

| Point | What a 10 looks like |
|---|---|
| Point of view | A clear personality built on an idea from the brand's world. The signature treatment could not be swapped onto a competitor unchanged. |
| Typography | Confident composition and scale, purposeful fonts and hierarchy. Principal hero type visibly travels, scales or changes orientation with identifiable cues in the actual film, then settles for reading. Phone choreography is authored; semantics, emphasis and static fallbacks remain intact. Generic entrances or fades alone do not pass. |
| Color | A coherent palette sampled from the visual world, purposeful contrasts and an accent reserved for useful emphasis. |
| Hierarchy | Every screen has a deliberate focus and a clear next action. The page varies scale and density, repeats only for a reason, and sustains interest through the ending. |
| Imagery | Art-directed pictures where the direction calls for them, with consistent identity, light and materials. No accidental duplication, stray marks or obvious generation defects. The hero tells a composed story, not merely a camera drift; an image-light page is judged on the purpose and quality of its selected imagery, not quantity. |
| Motion | Image, type and interaction feel like one experience. The recorded film-cue/type relationships hold in desktop and phone playback, reverse scroll and arbitrary seeks, with the same state at the same position. Full film and joins flow; supporting moments sustain the story without overwhelming it. Effects load and run efficiently. |
| Mobile | The entire portrait story is composed, readable and reachable. Every beat and join survives, controls are easy to use, and the page loads well on a real connection. A crop is judged against a dedicated treatment where appropriate. |
| Polish | Fast loading, no console errors or layout jumps, truthful copy, working navigation and form states, keyboard access and composed video-failure/reduced-motion fallbacks. |

At the early visual-treatment review, note the creative questions the hero, lower section and phone view resolve. At the full first preview, show the scorecard and choose the three weakest points that most affect the experience. Name one concrete improvement for each, then complete the polish pass within the approved production plan.

Include settled crops of every lower section at relevant wide, ultrawide and phone widths, after fonts, media and entrances resolve. Identify its visual or explanatory focus and the next useful step, without requiring decoration or a CTA in every section. Record an independent critique of the weakest non-hero section when a reviewer is available; otherwise record a separate critical self-review. Include one relevant alternative and the reason for the chosen treatment. Complete improvements within the accepted scope autonomously; typography, whitespace and a strong hero alone cannot justify a high whole-page score.

For typography and motion, cite the selected film version and observed cue times alongside the corresponding type movement. For tracked type, cite the `track.mjs` proof sheets and the `typesync.mjs` result. Review the actual synchronized sequence and reading plateaus, including rapid/reverse seeks, phone framing and static fallbacks. A storyboard, screenshot, animated text alone or passing checker cannot certify that type moves with the film. Repair a missing or broken relationship before calling the cinematic experience complete.

After polishing, present both columns side by side:

| Point | First | After | Evidence of the change or remaining limitation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Typography | 7 | 9 | The headline now follows the reveal, then holds; wide and phone motion reviewed. |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Total | /80 | /80 | ... |

These are design judgments, not objective certifications. Keep screenshots, film reviews and relevant checks in `review/`. Explain any unchanged score honestly, and identify provisional assets by version rather than presenting them as approved final work. A Lighthouse score is evidence about its measured categories, not proof of creative quality, live email delivery or search-engine indexing. Do not claim physical-phone verification when only emulation was available; ask the user for the final real-device reaction without making them perform technical QA.
