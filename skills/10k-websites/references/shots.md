# The Shot Library: heroes that feel like a $10,000 film

Read this in Phase 3 (concepts) and again in Phase 6 (keyframes and video). These shots are inspiration, not a fixed menu. Invent or combine approaches when the brand calls for it, and test the result against the same story, composition and continuity standards. The selected production level controls exploration resources; it never obliges every brand to use the same reliable shot.

## Why most AI heroes look cheap

A camera that slowly dollies or tilts inside a room shows the room's edges, the wall, the corner, the ceiling line, and reads as a real estate walkthrough. Nothing happens, so nothing is remembered. The heroes that stop people scrolling do one of two things: **the camera goes somewhere** (it dives, falls, flies through, circles), or **something transforms** in front of it (it pours, assembles, unveils, grows). The best do both at once: the camera follows the coffee as it falls into the cup. And the ones people share tell a tiny story: something grabs the eye, something big happens, and it lands.

## The wow gate (every concept must pass, before you show it)

Score each concept yes or no. Show only concepts with five yeses.
1. **The camera travels or the subject transforms,** in one continuous journey. Not a slow drift, not a pan across a wall.
2. **Scale or state changes a lot** between the first frame and the last: sky to doorstep, macro to whole, apart to assembled, empty to full, hidden to revealed.
3. **The motion agrees with the scroll.** Scrolling down reads as falling, descending, pouring, arriving, opening. Never rising away from the visitor.
4. **It ends on a composed, poster-worthy frame** that can become the top of the website, with calm space for the header and one line of words.
5. **Its difficult details have a credible production plan.** Liquids, atmosphere, materials and architecture often work well. Faces, hands, familiar anatomy and complex products need stronger identity references, capable models and explicit inspection. Compose around a limitation when that helps the concept; do not automatically exclude the subject that makes the brand distinctive.

If the business is a room (a hotel suite, a salon), the room is the destination, never the whole journey: descend from the sky to the building, or fly in through the window, and let the room be the final frame.

## The story gate (the difference between a camera move and a film)

A shot that passes the wow gate is still only one move. The heroes people remember tell a tiny story in three beats. Check every concept for all three before you pitch it:
1. **The opening grabs the eye by itself.** The first frame is already a picture worth a screenshot: raw materials in dramatic light, the world from an impossible height, the product in pieces mid-air. Never a plainer version of the ending.
2. **Something happens in the middle.** An event with energy: parts rush in and slam together, liquid crashes into a crown of splash, the camera bursts out of the clouds, a flash turns one thing into another. The EVENT keyframe shows that moment at its peak.
3. **The reveal lands.** The last frame is the poster, and it feels earned because of what came before.

**The screenshot test:** picture the three keyframes as thumbnails side by side. If any two look like the same photo cropped differently, the story is too small. Each is a different shot: a different camera position, a different scale, a different state.

**The friend test:** could a visitor describe the hero to a friend in one sentence that sounds exciting? "The headphones burst into a hundred parts and slam back together right in front of you." If the sentence is "the camera moves toward the product", start again.

## The shots

Adapt one, combine compatible ideas, or propose an original journey. Each example lists who it suits and three story frames: OPEN (the first frame), EVENT (the big moment, at its peak) and REST (the frame the page rests on). These are narrative beats, not a mandatory two-clip structure. The paired camera prompts illustrate a chained version; a continuous take can carry both beats. Choose which frames are generation inputs and which are guides using "The keyframe and take method" below.

### 1. The Pour
Coffee, tea, wine, spirits, cocktails, perfume, serums, honey, chocolate, sauces, candles, paint, beer.
Story: the first drops leave the source high above, the stream crashes into the vessel in a crown of splash, and the finished drink settles into a poster.
- OPEN: a close-up of the source (the espresso machine's spouts, the bottle's neck, the ladle) with the first drops forming in hard backlight, the vessel far below and out of frame.
- EVENT: the impact, seen low and close: the stream hitting the liquid, a crown of splash and droplets frozen in the light.
- REST: the finished vessel or product, settled, composed low in the frame with calm space above, liquid still glossy, fine droplets or steam in the light.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera races down alongside the falling stream, all the way from the spouts to the cup"; clip 2 "the splash rises and falls back as the camera pulls back and lowers to rest on the finished cup".

### 2. The Descent
Hotels, resorts, cabins, real estate, venues, wineries, farms, campuses, cities, ski and golf.
Story: from high above the clouds, the camera plunges through the cloud layer, bursts out over the valley, and sweeps in to land at the front door.
- OPEN: a sea of clouds at golden hour from far above, a tiny warm glow where the building is.
- EVENT: just below the clouds, mist streaming off the lens, the whole valley and the building revealed far below, the camera diving steeply.
- REST: the building at human height, lights warm, a path leading in, sky calm above.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera dives forward and down through the clouds, white mist rushing across the lens"; clip 2 "the camera sweeps low along the valley and glides in to settle in front of the building". The one-clip version landed first try.

### 3. The Assembly
Tech products, headphones, watches, cameras, sneakers, furniture, bikes, cosmetics packaging, burgers and layered food, app screens as floating layers.
Story: every part hangs scattered through the air around the camera, the parts rush in from every side, and they slam together into the finished product.
- OPEN: a wide exploded view in darkness, parts spread across the whole frame, some so close to the lens they are soft and huge, small screws hanging between them, hard beams of light cutting through.
- EVENT: the parts mid-flight and converging, halfway together, motion in every direction, small sparks of light where edges meet, the camera swinging around them.
- REST: the finished product in a three-quarter view, rim-lit, small enough in the frame to leave generous space around it (phones crop the sides).
- Cables: leave them out of the OPEN and EVENT frames, or show one clean separate coil. A loose cable among flying parts never makes sense to an image model (a Halden test needed three redos before removing the cable fixed it). The cable can appear on the REST frame.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the parts rush inward from every side of the frame as the camera swings around them"; clip 2 "the last parts lock together with precise, satisfying mechanical motion and the camera settles on the finished product". The one-clip version landed first try.

### 4. The Fly-through
Restaurants, bars, shops, studios, experiences, museums, event venues.
Story: from across the street, the camera races toward the glowing doorway, bursts through the glass in a flare, and glides to rest at the best table.
- OPEN: well outside and far back (across the street, at the end of the path, over the water), the whole facade small in the frame and the inside glowing through it. Starting at the doorstep makes a plain push-in, the dull dolly this library exists to avoid (a restaurant build hit it).
- EVENT: the threshold, the doorway filling the frame, a warm flare off the glass, the room opening up beyond.
- REST: the inside world, composed and lit, the most beautiful table or corner in the middle distance.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera glides fast across the street toward the open doorway"; clip 2 "the camera passes through the doorway in a soft flare and comes to rest inside". Keep the inside simple: one room, one light direction.

### 5. The Pull-back Reveal
Jewellery, watches, fashion, textiles, makers, woodwork, ceramics, architecture materials, skincare, food.
Story: an extreme close-up of a material fills the screen, the camera rips back as light flares off an edge, and the whole object appears in its world.
- OPEN: a macro of the product's most beautiful material, filling the frame, with soft hints of the whole (the curve of the band, the edge of the setting) so it reads as one object.
- EVENT: mid pull-back, the object half-revealed, light flaring off an edge, focus racing.
- REST: the whole product or scene, composed.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera pulls back fast from the macro detail as a flare sweeps across the edge"; clip 2 "the camera keeps rising and settles on the whole piece, focus sliding from the texture to the object".

### 6. The Unveil
Luxury goods, cars, perfume, launches, fashion, art.
Story: the product waits hidden under silk in one hard light, a gust tears the silk away, and the product stands in full light.
- OPEN: the product hidden under flowing silk (or behind thick smoke), only its silhouette readable, one hard light raking across it.
- EVENT: the silk mid-flight, whipping away and down across the frame, the product half-revealed and catching the light.
- REST: the product fully lit on a plinth or in its setting, with the silk pooled at its base or trailing off the plinth edge. The cloth needs somewhere to land, or the video model makes it vanish.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the silk is torn away at once and whips down across the frame as the camera pushes in"; clip 2 "the silk falls the rest of the way and pools at the base as the light rises on the product".
- Watch the clip for the cloth vanishing in one frame instead of falling. That is this shot's failure mode (a perfume build hit it).

### 7. The Orbit Down
Cars, sculpture, buildings, cakes, perfume bottles, anything on a plinth.
Story: the camera starts straight above the object, sweeps down around it in a big arc as the light flares past, and lands on the hero view.
- OPEN: a straight top-down view of the object and its ground, with one long dramatic shadow.
- EVENT: the camera mid-arc at about 45 degrees, a low sun flaring past the object's edge.
- REST: an eye-level three-quarter hero view.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera swoops down and around the object in a fast, sweeping arc"; clip 2 "the arc continues to eye level and settles on the three-quarter view".

### 8. The Splash
Pools, spas, swimwear, drinks, surf schools, boats, hydration and beauty.
Story: the camera drops from above the water, smashes through the surface, and settles in the light below.
- OPEN: high above the water, the subject or the pool from above, light glittering on the surface.
- EVENT: the impact: the lens breaking the surface, a wall of splash and droplets on the lens.
- REST: the underwater or half-underwater view, light rays, the subject clear.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the camera drops fast toward the water and smashes through the surface in a burst of splash"; clip 2 "a beat of blur and bubbles, then the camera settles underwater in the light rays".

### 9. The Light Tunnel
Software, AI, fintech, music, events, agencies, anything invisible.
Story: the camera flies into a tunnel of light, rings stream past faster and faster, and it arrives at the product or the brand's world.
- OPEN: the tunnel's entrance, a single point of light far ahead.
- EVENT: deep inside, rings of light streaming past at speed, the colour shifting.
- REST: the product (a device, a stage, a glowing object) resolved and still.
- Camera words: "the camera keeps flying forward the whole time, passing ring after ring as the light streams past, and arrives only in the final second". Abstract worlds can reduce identity constraints, but their joins still need inspection.
- Watch for early arrival: when two keyframes look alike (the same tunnel), the model may reach the end frame early and hold still, making part of the scroll dead. Strengthen the distance between story beats and specify continuing motion. If the hold persists, reconsider the endpoint or take structure rather than repeating the same prompt.

### 10. The Rise
Landscaping, gardening, construction, bakeries, brewing, anything that grows.
Story: the thing grows or builds in front of the camera in a rush of motion while the camera circles up.
- OPEN: the seed bed, the empty plot, the raw dough, in the same place and light.
- EVENT: mid-growth: stems unfurling, walls rising, the dough swelling, light sweeping across.
- REST: the grown garden, the finished building, the risen loaf.
- Higher risk (a subject changing its shape). Keep the change continuous. Price one extra re-roll in.

### 11. The Transformation
Perfume, drinks, cosmetics, fashion, food, tech launches, and any brand with a raw-material story: beans become the cup, clay becomes the vase, a wave of sound becomes the headphones, a single drop becomes the bottle.
Story: the raw thing rushes toward the camera, bursts into spray, light or particles that fill the screen, and the finished product forms out of the burst.
- OPEN: the raw thing, beautiful on its own: a single drop of perfume falling through the dark, coffee beans tumbling in a beam of light, a ripple of sound drawn in light.
- EVENT: the burst at its peak, the frame filled with spray, particles, petals or light, the first edges of the product just visible inside it.
- REST: the finished product, whole and still, in the same light, with the last particles settling around it.
- Camera words: clip 1 "the drop falls toward the camera and explodes into a burst of golden spray that fills the frame"; clip 2 "the spray swirls and draws together into the bottle, and the last droplets settle as the camera eases to rest".
- Why it works: no clip ever has to turn one object into another in plain view. Clip 1 ends inside the burst and clip 2 starts inside it, so the swap happens behind the spray. Keep one heading and one speed through the burst. This is the shot that makes people ask how it was made, and it needs a re-roll more often than the others: price one in.

### 12. The Pick-up
Headphones, watches, bags, shoes, sunglasses, cameras, phones, bottles, jewellery: anything people wear or hold. Recommend it first for products with complex parts, where exploding or morphing the product fails.
Story: the product rests in beautiful light, a hand lifts it out of frame, and the film lands on the person out in their world with it, wearing it or carrying it.
- OPEN: the product at rest on a surface that fits the brand (a desk, a shelf, a café table), still and whole, in strong light. Use the user's real photo as an identity reference when needed, while explicitly designing this composition.
- EVENT: the lift, close and fast: a hand grabbing the product with motion blur, the background already streaking. Keep the hand small or blurred.
- REST: the person out in the world (a city street at dusk, a train platform, a studio) wearing or holding the product, seen from behind or in profile, face never shown, the product clear and in focus, calm space for the header.
- Camera words: clip 1 "a hand reaches in and lifts the headphones in one quick motion, the camera following the lift"; clip 2 "the motion carries through a blur and the camera settles behind the person on the street, the headphones on their head, city lights behind".
- Why it works: nothing about the product has to change shape, and the story is one anyone can read in a second. It was the best film of every Halden test.

## The keyframe and take method

Design OPEN, EVENT and REST, then choose the take structure from the required motion and the selected model's actual controls. A continuous take can use OPEN and REST as inputs with EVENT as a timed guide; it avoids an internal endpoint but may reproduce that middle composition less precisely. A chain can pin EVENT between clips when separate state control or a designed transition is more useful. Neither method is automatically cheaper, better or more ambitious. An endpoint image constrains appearance, not camera speed: a shared picture does not guarantee matching generated endpoints, texture or velocity.

Record a **route contract**: starting height/distance and bearing, direction of travel, expected foreground parallax, any traversable gap or boundary, and arrival bearing. State where motion must continue and where it may decelerate. For subject-led shots, describe the equivalent path and state change. Attractive stills alone do not prove this route. Test an unproven ambitious route with a representative motion pilot before dependent clips and formats, within the approved allowance; apply the pilot and acceptance protocol in [visual-validation.md](visual-validation.md).

1. **Generate the REST frame first** (text to image, or the user's polished real photo). It is the most important picture on the site: the page comes to rest on it and it becomes the poster. Repeat it lower down only for a deliberate narrative purpose. Compose it with calm space for the header and one line of words.
2. **Make every earlier frame a new shot, with the right identity support.** Describe the recognizable shape, materials, colours and signature details, then the new camera position, distance, angle, scale and subject state. Use a real product or approved frame as an identity reference immediately when consistency matters; do not spend two failed generations before allowing it. Assign references explicit roles: scene identity, composition, or material/detail quality. Use one composition authority for a local repair; additional views must not compete to define its camera. Where supported, use separate controls or masks. Text-only generation is appropriate when the subject is generic or references would over-constrain the shot. Reject copied composition and identity drift separately, using the scene contract and reference checks in [visual-validation.md](visual-validation.md).
3. **Check the set side by side** before showing it:
   **The screenshot test:** as thumbnails, the frames look like different shots. If the OPEN frame looks like the REST frame re-cropped or slightly tilted, the move is too small and the hero will feel like a dolly: redo it much further away, from another angle, or in another state.
   **The earlier-state check:** each frame shows its own moment: parts still apart, silk still covering, the cup still empty, the door still ahead. Say the state in the prompt ("the cup below is still empty and clean").
   **The energy check:** the EVENT frame is the most dramatic picture of the set: motion in progress, splash at its peak, light flaring, things mid-air. A calm middle frame makes a calm film.
   **The framing check:** scale serves the beat. A distant OPEN may make the destination small to establish real travel; its context must still make the route legible. EVENT gives the action enough presence to read, and REST shows the defining details at delivery size. Do not apply one minimum subject-size ratio to every beat.
   **The one-world check:** the same product design, the same palette, the same kind of ground and background, and light that belongs to one film. Fix a frame now if it drifts. A still costs a fraction of a video. In the Halden test, an EVENT frame on a stone floor after an OPEN frame in a dark void made the first clip melt in the middle.
   **The foreground check:** a close, optically soft object may provide useful parallax or occlusion, but its approach, attachment and exit must be physically coherent. Reject abrupt edge intrusions, unexplained pop-ins and blur that substitutes for the promised event; do not ban deliberate near-lens passes outright.
4. **Review the set** in story order under the current approval/delegation policy in `SKILL.md`. User-delegated creative review still requires the same evidence; it does not add another approval request.
5. **Inspect each complete clip in motion and as frames** before presenting it. A contact sheet cannot establish timing or continuity. Diagnose **the dead half** (early arrival or an intermediate stop: check beat separation, endpoint constraints and take structure), **the vanish** (an object disappears instead of moving: give it a coherent destination), **the small push** (little travel: revise the actual route and source framing), and **the smear** (parts lose identity: inspect references, geometry and the transition before choosing a repair). A stronger adjective or another identical retry is not a diagnosis. Apply the evidence checks in [visual-validation.md](visual-validation.md), preserving approved scenes and pace during local repairs; only confirmed duplicate-frame removal uses the timing exception in `ffmpeg-recipes.md`.

Add further story frames only when the journey needs them; their number does not dictate the number of clips. Only the final arrival needs a resting composition. A middle frame should depict continuing action, but a picture of action does not prove that the generated camera will still be moving there.

## Writing the prompts

**Keyframe (REST), the frame the page rests on:**
```
[Genre: luxury product commercial / architectural photograph / ...]. [SUBJECT in its
finished state], [where it sits in the frame], [what the light does on it].
[The world around it as materials and light, the brand palette as real things].
[Camera: height, lens feel, depth of field]. Calm space [above / to the left] for a
headline. Photorealistic, cinematic colour grade, 16:9. No unrequested text or logos.
Preserve approved product marks.
```

**Keyframes (OPEN and EVENT), each a distinct composition with identity references when needed:**
```
[Genre, the same as the REST frame]. [THE PRODUCT IN WORDS: shape, materials, colours,
the signature detail], [ITS STATE AT THIS MOMENT]. The camera is [NEW POSITION, DISTANCE
AND ANGLE, clearly different from the final frame]. The frame is full of [THE ENERGY:
spray frozen mid-air / parts flying / a hand lifting it in motion blur]. [THE WORLD in
the same palette and colour of light as the final frame]. Photorealistic, cinematic,
16:9. No unrequested text or logos. Preserve approved product marks.
```

**Video, one prompt per clip:**
```
One continuous shot, no cuts. [CAMERA PATH with a strong verb: the camera dives /
races down alongside the stream / swings around / bursts through]. [THE ACTION with
energy: the parts rush inward from every side / the spray explodes outward].
[AMBIENT LIFE: sparks, spray, streaming cloud, light flares]. [BOUNDARY MOMENT if
crossed: mist across the lens, a splash and droplets]. [PACE: fast and powerful /
sweeping and decisive / building momentum]. The shot [ends exactly on the final frame,
still in motion / lands and comes to rest exactly on the final frame]. No unrequested
text or lettering. Preserve approved product marks.
```

**Pace:** give every clip energy. The first clip usually builds (fast, sweeping, powerful) and the last one lands (decisive, then settling exactly on the final frame). "Slow and gentle" makes a screensaver: use it only when the brand is truly calm, and even then keep the camera travelling.

**Words that break shots:**
- Never name the camera's hardware: "drone", "FPV", "gimbal", "crane", "dolly track". Models draw the device into the picture (a real test showed drone propellers in frame). Say "the camera".
- A difficult identity change can happen behind a motivated burst in either a continuous take or a chain. A plain-view transformation requires evidence that the selected model can preserve the intended anatomy and identity.
- Avoid "zoom" (it flattens). Say the camera moves: "pushes in", "pulls back", "dives".
- Describe one coherent pace curve, with observable beat timing where supported. Smooth acceleration and final deceleration can belong within one take. Avoid contradictory speed commands; continuing segments must maintain compatible direction and speed across the join.

## Models and production resources

Follow the live selection and spending policy in `SKILL.md` and `openart.md`. Record the selected `production_level`, the current GPT model, and separate image and video model choices. Full throttle uses the strongest current GPT available for the work throughout and the strongest current suitable photo/video models at high-quality settings. Do not silently fall back to a smaller assistant or cheaper generation model.

Evaluate the latest Seedance family or successor among the leading available video candidates, then choose on current evidence for this shot: motion, identity, references, detail and start/end control. A historical comparison, a higher price, or a familiar model name does not establish today's winner. Keep prior build results as clues, not defaults. Use the approved allowance for meaningful creative alternatives and targeted repairs; more generations are useful only when they improve the result.

## Review the assembled film

Keep rendering, inspecting and presenting results without waiting for the user to ask whether the job is finished. Use `node .10k/tools/review.mjs review/hero-joined.mp4 --port 8081` to present normal playback, seeking, half speed and frame stepping when inline review is unreliable. Inspect the entire film, then every join in both directions, including portrait. Record approved filenames and durations so later repairs preserve the approved film. See `ffmpeg-recipes.md` for matched cuts, duplicate endpoint handling and carefully checked blends.

## Captions and the shot

Plan where the words sit while the motion happens. Falling and diving shots keep a vertical action lane in the middle, so words flank it left and right. Assembly and orbit shots keep the product in the middle and the words above or beside it. The headline sits over the OPEN frame's calmest area, the EVENT gets few or no words so the motion leads, and the last line sits over the REST frame's calm space. Plan how each line moves with the shot, then settles for reading; preserve stable controls and a composed reduced-motion state.
